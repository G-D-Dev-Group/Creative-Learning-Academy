# Social Studies (TEA Chapter 113)

STAAR-tested: grade 8, plus the U.S. History end-of-course exam.

Every grade uses the same eight strands: History, Geography, Economics, Government, Citizenship, Culture, Science/Technology & Society, and Social Studies Skills. What changes each year is the **focus** -- and Texas has a very distinctive, deliberate grade-by-grade sequence:

**K-3** self/family/community -> **4** Texas history -> **5** early U.S. history -> **6** world cultures/geography -> **7** Texas history (in depth) -> **8** U.S. history to 1877 -> **9/10** World Geography & World History -> **11** U.S. History since 1877 -> **12** Government (semester) + Economics (semester).

## Kindergarten -- Self, Home, Family, Classroom
Patriotic holidays; figures like Stephen F. Austin and George Washington; spatial vocabulary; needs vs. wants; purpose of rules; family traditions.

## Grade 1 -- Classroom, School, Community
Origins of customs/holidays; cardinal directions; goods and services, scarcity; rules and laws; inventors (Bell, Edison, Garrett Morgan).

## Grade 2 -- The Local Community
Community/state/national celebrations; continents and oceans; earning/spending/saving; government functions; citizenship exemplars (Thurgood Marshall, Paul Revere).

## Grade 3 -- People Who Changed Communities and the World
Community founders; regional adaptation (deserts, mountains, wetlands); free enterprise basics, supply and demand; local/state/national government structure.

## Grade 4 -- Texas History (early beginnings to present)
American Indian groups (Caddo, Karankawa, Jumano); explorers; Texas Revolution (the Alamo, San Jacinto); the Republic era; cattle industry and the oil boom; the four Texas geographic regions; Spanish colonial and Mexican government; the six flags of Texas.

## Grade 5 -- U.S. History (1565 to present)
Colonization; the Revolutionary War and Founding Fathers; the Constitution; Manifest Destiny; the Civil War and Reconstruction; 20th-century civil rights (MLK, Rosa Parks, Cesar Chavez); the Mayflower Compact, Declaration of Independence, Bill of Rights, checks and balances.

## Grade 6 -- World Cultures and Geography (contemporary world)
How colonization, immigration, and trade shape modern societies; population patterns; factors of production; free enterprise vs. socialist vs. communist systems; limited vs. unlimited government; religious traditions worldwide.

## Grade 7 -- Texas History (in-depth, early times to present)
Natural Texas through Contemporary Texas eras; Mexican independence; the Texas Revolution in depth; Civil War/Reconstruction in Texas; the cattle, railroad, and oil eras; the Texas Constitution vs. the U.S. Constitution.

## Grade 8 STAAR-tested -- U.S. History (colonial period through Reconstruction, to 1877)
Causes of the Revolution (Stamp Act, Intolerable Acts); the Constitutional Convention (the Great Compromise, Three-Fifths Compromise); the Jackson era (Trail of Tears); the Civil War (slavery as the central cause); Reconstruction; the plantation system and the transatlantic slave trade; Marbury v. Madison, Dred Scott v. Sandford.

## Grades 9/10 -- World Geography Studies
Climate systems, natural hazards, migration; traditional/free-enterprise/socialist/communist economic systems; major world religions; forms of government (democracy, dictatorship, monarchy, theocracy); the UN and EU.

## Grades 9/10 -- World History Studies
River-valley civilizations through the 20th century; classical Greece and Rome; the Renaissance and Reformation; Maya/Inca/Aztec civilizations; the Industrial Revolution and imperialism; WWI, WWII, the Cold War; political philosophers (Locke, Hobbes, Montesquieu).

*(Texas districts choose whether World Geography or World History comes first -- the TEKS define the content of each course, not the sequencing between them.)*

## Grade 11 STAAR EOC-tested -- U.S. History Since 1877
The Spanish-American War, WWI, the Great Depression, WWII, the Cold War; the civil rights movement through the 21st century; the Sherman Antitrust Act, the New Deal; Watergate; Plessy v. Ferguson, Brown v. Board; the Harlem Renaissance.

## Grade 12 (semester) -- U.S. Government
The Constitution's underlying principles: republicanism, federalism, checks and balances, separation of powers; the 19th/24th/26th Amendments; political parties, interest groups, media, and voting; the Federalist Papers and landmark case analysis.

## Grade 12 (semester) -- Economics with Emphasis on Free Enterprise
Scarcity and opportunity cost; supply and demand; market structures (monopoly, oligopoly); personal financial literacy (credit, loans, bankruptcy, insurance, FAFSA); the Federal Reserve, fiscal policy, and taxation.

## How this progresses, K-12
The sequence is deliberately concentric: it starts at the personal/family scale (K-2), widens to the local community (3), then alternates between **Texas-specific** history (4, 7) and **U.S.** history (5, 8, 11) with a **world** history/geography year in between (6, 9/10) -- before culminating in the civic/economic frameworks (government and economics) a graduating senior needs as an adult citizen (12). Skills escalate in parallel: simple map-reading and rule-following (K-2) -> cause-and-effect historical narrative (3-8) -> primary-source analysis, political cartoon interpretation, and historiography (9-12).
