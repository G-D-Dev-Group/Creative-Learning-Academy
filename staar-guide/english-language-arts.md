# English Language Arts & Reading (TEA Chapter 110)

STAAR-tested: grades 3-8 (Reading/Language Arts), plus English I and English II end-of-course exams.

## The strands (constant K-12)

Every grade and course is organized around the same core strands -- only the complexity changes:
1. **Foundational Language Skills** -- phonics, fluency, vocabulary (phased out as a distinct strand after elementary; folded into vocabulary/fluency work in secondary)
2. **Comprehension Skills**
3. **Response Skills**
4. **Multiple Genres** -- literary elements and genre characteristics
5. **Author's Purpose and Craft**
6. **Composition** -- the writing process and genres of writing
7. **Inquiry and Research**

## Kindergarten
- **Foundational:** phonological awareness (rhyme, syllables), decoding simple CVC words, 25+ high-frequency words, letter formation
- **Comprehension/Response:** predictions with adult support, personal connections, oral/drawn responses
- **Genres:** folktales, fables, fairy tales, nursery rhymes; basic informational text
- **Composition:** dictate/compose simple narratives and informational texts, mostly through drawing
- **Inquiry:** adult-guided question generation

## Grade 1
- **Foundational:** blends/digraphs, common spelling patterns, 100+ high-frequency words
- **Comprehension:** questioning before/during/after reading, inferring with evidence
- **Genres:** adds drama elements, persuasive-text purpose
- **Composition:** narratives, poetry, informational/procedural texts, simple correspondence
- **Inquiry:** research plans with support

## Grade 2
- **Foundational:** multisyllabic decoding, common prefixes, cursive introduced
- **Vocabulary:** context clues beyond the sentence, antonyms/synonyms/idioms
- **Genres:** fact vs. opinion, digital/multimodal text
- **Composition:** adds informational reports
- **Inquiry:** identifies primary vs. secondary sources, begins citing sources

## Grade 3 STAAR-tested
- **Foundational:** advanced multisyllabic patterns, compound words/contractions
- **Comprehension:** annotating when comprehension breaks down
- **Genres:** legends/myths added; argumentative text (claim vs. fact/opinion) introduced
- **Composition:** opinion essays with argumentative structure
- **Inquiry:** paraphrasing vs. plagiarism, works-cited pages

## Grade 4 STAAR-tested
- **Vocabulary:** Greek/Latin roots begin, advanced affixes
- **Genres:** tall tales, figurative language (simile/metaphor/personification), drama structure
- **Author's Craft:** sound devices (alliteration, assonance)
- **Composition:** argumentative essays with factual support
- **Inquiry:** bibliographies, expanded primary/secondary source use

## Grade 5 STAAR-tested
- **Vocabulary:** adages, puns, more root families
- **Genres:** multiple-theme inference, character conflict analysis
- **Author's Craft:** hyperbole, point-of-view analysis
- **Composition:** argumentative texts with claims + evidence
- **Inquiry:** source reliability begins emerging

## Grade 6 STAAR-tested (entry to middle school)
- **Vocabulary:** systematic Greek/Latin root study
- **Genres:** informational text organizational patterns; argument audience awareness
- **Author's Craft:** omniscient/limited point of view; rhetorical devices vs. logical fallacies
- **Composition:** multi-paragraph essays with a clear thesis
- **Inquiry:** source reliability, credibility, bias, faulty reasoning; academic citation begins

## Grade 7 STAAR-tested
- **Genres:** adds fantasy/science fiction; foreshadowing/suspense analysis
- **Author's Craft:** subjective/objective point of view; rhetorical devices (direct address) vs. fallacies (loaded language)
- **Composition:** subordinating/correlative conjunctions, more complex sentence structure
- **Inquiry:** refining research questions, evaluating alternative arguments

## Grade 8 STAAR-tested
- **Genres:** non-linear plot (flashbacks, subplots, parallel structure)
- **Author's Craft:** extended metaphor, irony, multiple points of view; fallacies (bandwagon, circular reasoning) -- the most complex fallacy set before high school
- **Composition:** active/passive voice, advanced punctuation (colons, nonrestrictive clauses)
- **Inquiry:** counter-argument analysis, bias by omission

## High School -- English I through IV
Same seven strands; the verbs used in the standards escalate: **understand -> analyze -> evaluate -> critique**.

**English I** **STAAR EOC-tested:** denotative/connotative vocabulary; character foils, non-linear plots; irony, understatement/overstatement; literary/informational/argumentative writing plus professional correspondence; research credibility and bias evaluation.

**English II** **STAAR EOC-tested:** adds figurative-meaning analysis; cross-cultural theme comparison; rhetorical appeals, antithesis, parallelism; research fallacies (hasty generalization, either-or).

**English III:** responses shift to full "analysis" level; moral dilemma/motivation and historical/social context; evaluates structure and devices (paradox, satire, allegory); adds literary and rhetorical analysis, resumes; research fallacies (post hoc, circular reasoning, red herring).

**English IV:** responses reach the "evaluative" tier; classical rhetoric and argument formulation; "critique and evaluate" language throughout; the most advanced fallacy set (straw man, false dilemma, non sequitur).

**High school electives:** Independent Study in English, Reading I/II/III (remediation/support), College Readiness and Study Skills, Visual Media Analysis and Production, Contemporary Media, Literary Genres, Creative Writing, Research and Technical Writing, Practical Writing Skills.

## How this progresses, K-12
- **Phonics** exists as a distinct strand only K-5, then folds into vocabulary/fluency work.
- **Vocabulary** moves from picture-based meaning (K) -> root/affix study (4-8) -> denotative/connotative/figurative and foreign-phrase analysis (high school).
- **Genres** move from folktales/fables (K-2) -> myths/tall tales (3-5) -> realistic fiction/sci-fi/fantasy (6-8) -> literary/rhetorical analysis (high school).
- **Argumentation** moves from fact-vs-opinion (2-3) -> claims+evidence (4-5) -> audience/counter-argument (6-8) -> classical rhetoric and an expanding logical-fallacy vocabulary every year through English IV.
- **Research** moves from adult-guided gathering (K-2) -> citation and plagiarism awareness (3-5) -> bias/credibility/fallacy evaluation (6-8) -> sophisticated academic synthesis (high school).
